# Market

Use this on every job. Measured figures live in `benchmarks.md`. Do not paste them here.

## Name the audience before you write

A usable audience has four parts: city or district, language, life context, and who pays. "India", "millennials", and "Bharat" are not audiences.

Tier is a clue, not a personality.

- Metro (examples: Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, Pune): denser English and Hinglish on social, more quick commerce, still a household behind many purchases.
- Tier 2 and Tier 3 (examples: Jaipur, Lucknow, Indore, Surat, Coimbatore, Nagpur, Vadodara): growth and premiumisation are real, and these cities are not one segment. A Lucknow brief and an Indore brief can share a film and still need different pins, retailers, and festival weight.
- Rural: local language is a trust requirement. Hindi reaches many people. It does not replace Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Bengali, Gujarati, Punjabi, Odia, or Assamese where that is the home language.

If the brief names two cities, keep both. Do not average them into "North India".

## Language

Pick the language the way you pick a channel.

- Hinglish: urban social, youth, and many D2C brands, when the audience already talks that way. It is a fluent mix, not broken English with "ji" added.
- Full regional language: rural, high-consideration (health, finance, education, property), and any moment where trust matters more than reach.
- English: the CA console, some B2B, and premium metro work. The owner's app, the WhatsApp reply, and the pack line often still need Hindi or the state language.

Translation swaps words. Localisation changes the occasion, the kitchen, the shop, and the joke. A national proposition can stay national. The film often cannot.

## Who decides

Many categories split three roles: who pays, who uses, and who is asked before the purchase. Family and close friends close a lot of new-brand decisions after the feed has done the introduction. Write the role into the brief. A snack bought for guests is not the same job as a snack the buyer eats alone.

## Price and path

Design the offer as a ladder, not a single "affordable" claim.

- Small entry packs and round rupee prices (₹10, ₹20, ₹99, ₹299) when the category is impulse or gifting.
- Family packs when the household is the user.
- EMI, UPI, and cash on delivery when the ticket or the trust is high. COD is a trust tool. It is also a cost. Do not promise it unless the brief says the business can do it.
- Kirana and general trade, modern trade, marketplaces (Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho), and quick commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart). Name the path that exists today. Do not invent a pin code you can deliver to by evening.

Salary cycles (around the 1st, and in some workplaces the 7th or 10th) and festival weeks change when people spend. The calendar file handles timing.

## Tone

Write to adults. Aspiration is specific: a better host, a cleaner shop, a CA who leaves on time in filing week, a parent who understood the school form.

Do not write any of these:

- Mock pidgin, or English with random Hindi words to signal "mass".
- A village costume, a dialect joke, or one stock "Indian kitchen" for every state.
- Pity, or a metro narrator explaining Bharat to itself.
- Cricket, cinema, or a wedding dropped in because the brief says India.

## Company types

Name which one you are serving: D2C, FMCG, MSME, startup, enterprise, clinic, coaching, real estate, or a creator selling their own product. The buyer set changes with the type. A clinic and a marketplace brand do not share a claim rule or a channel plan.
