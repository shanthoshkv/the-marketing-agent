# Compliance

Apply these checks to the script, the caption, the landing page, and the WhatsApp reply. This is marketing guidance, not legal advice. Confirm a date or a penalty against the Gazette or the regulator before you quote it to a client.

Do not write election persuasion or communal creative. Refuse a brief that asks for either, and say why in one line.

## What has to be true before a claim ships

| Category | The line may ship when |
| --- | --- |
| Health, nutrition, AYUSH, drugs | The brand can show the evidence, and a qualified person says the technical part with that qualification on screen up front. An unqualified creator stays on generic, non-technical talk. |
| BFSI, investments, credit, insurance | Technical advice comes from a person with the real registration (SEBI for investment advice), shown up front. Returns are not guaranteed. |
| Education and jobs | Rank, score, placement, and income lines match a real, current result the brand can prove. No guaranteed selection. |
| Property | Area, price, approval, and possession match the documents. No "best" and no fake scarcity. |
| Tax and compliance software | Say "prepare" or "validate". Do not say "we file your return" or "no notice" unless a licensed workflow in the brief actually does that. |
| Anything | The superlative has a source. The price is the price they charge. The offer ends when the brief says it ends. |

ASCI Addendum II (April 2025) is the health and BFSI qualification rule. The CCPA's misleading-ad and endorsement guidelines sit on top of industry codes. Secondary write-ups cite penalties up to ₹10 lakh for individuals and ₹50 lakh for entities. Do not invent a penalty for a specific client.

## Disease and cure lines

Cut any line that says or implies a cure, a treatment, a prevention, or a time-bound medical result ("cures diabetes in 7 days", "sugar will be normal by Sunday") unless the evidence and the qualified speaker are in the brief. They almost never are.

Do not leave the cut line in the script as something the creator could still read. Name it once, in English, as the line you removed. Do not translate that line into Hindi or any other audience language.

Then write the shippable version:

- What the product is (food, cosmetic, or whatever the brief proves).
- Who it is for, in ordinary language.
- Taste, habit, ingredient, or ritual, only where the brief supports the ingredient.
- A clear "this is not a medicine" when a viewer could confuse the two.
- If a doctor or dietitian speaks, their qualification is the first thing on screen, and they stay inside what that qualification covers.

## Influencer disclosure

A material connection is pay, a gift, barter, affiliate commission, equity, employment, or family. Disclose it.

- Labels that pass: Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Paid Partnership. In the language of the ad. A Hindi film gets a Hindi disclosure, not an English #ad buried under the caption.
- The label sits where it is seen without expanding, scrolling, or watching to the end. Not inside a hashtag block. Not only in the bio.
- Video: say it early, and keep a text label on screen. A line that appears in the last seconds does not count.
- Live: say it at the start and repeat it.
- `#sp`, `#spon`, and `#collab` without the plain word do not count.
- The brand and the creator can both be responsible. Put the disclosure in the brief, not in a later email.

## Synthetic people and pictures

ASCI's synthetically generated content guidelines were published on 29 September 2026 and were reported to take effect about three months later. Label the work when a missing label could change the decision:

- An AI influencer or ambassador.
- A real person's face or voice cloned, even with consent.
- A setting or event that did not happen and that changes how the product is understood.
- A demo of a product that does not exist yet.
- Paid AI product recommendations: say who sponsored them.

A label does not make these acceptable: a fake testimonial, an exaggerated result, a fake place presented as real, unauthorised copyrighted material, or a person's likeness without consent.

Routine colour correction and AI-written copy are outside the picture-and-sound labelling rule. Say when a person in the film is synthetic.

## Data and messaging

- Promotional WhatsApp and promotional SMS need recorded, purpose-specific consent. Marketing is not a DPDP "legitimate use".
- SMS and commercial calls follow TRAI TCCCPR: DLT registration, approved templates, the right header. WhatsApp does not ride that DLT header regime; Meta's own opt-in rules and the DPDP Act still apply.
- DPDP Rules, 2025, Gazette G.S.R. 846(E), dated 13 November 2025. Board machinery from notification. Consent Manager registration (Rule 4) about one year later, commonly counted as 13 November 2026. Notice, security, breach, retention, and rights duties about eighteen months later, commonly counted as 13 May 2027. Some summaries use 14 November because a PIB note differs from the Gazette date. Check the Gazette before you tell a client which day.
- The opt-out is as easy to see as the opt-in. No pre-ticked box, no hidden withdrawal.

## Dark patterns

Do not write a fake countdown, a fake "only 3 left", a hidden opt-out, or a price that is not the price at checkout. If the scarcity is real, the brief has to say why.
